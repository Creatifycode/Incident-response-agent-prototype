## Summary

`orders-api` instances terminated with out-of-memory errors repeatedly between
02:47 and 04:12 UTC on 2026-03-04. Auto-scaling replaced crashed instances,
which extended the impact over 85 minutes. The root cause was an unbounded
in-memory cache introduced in deployment `orders-api v2.9.1` on 2026-03-03.
Service was restored at 04:12 UTC by rolling back to v2.9.0.

## Trigger and recent changes

`orders-api v2.9.1` was deployed on 2026-03-03 at 16:20 UTC, roughly ten hours
before the incident. The release added a product-detail cache intended to
reduce database load. The cache had no size bound and no time-to-live.

The cache only began causing failures once the working set exceeded available
heap. `orders-api` ran with a 2 GB heap limit. The cache filled gradually over
several hours of sustained traffic and the first OOM kill occurred at 02:47
UTC, during the overnight batch window when catalog lookups changed key
distribution.

## Symptoms

- `orders-api` pods terminated with `JavaScript heap out of memory` at
  02:47, 02:58, 03:14, 03:31, 03:49 and 04:05 UTC.
- Sustained pod restarts: 6 crashes across 85 minutes.
- Heap usage climbed monotonically from 400 MB at 02:00 UTC to 2 GB at the
  time of each kill.
- Upstream API gateway returned intermittent 502 responses.
- `payments-api` unaffected throughout.

## Root cause

An unbounded in-memory product-detail cache introduced in `orders-api v2.9.1`.
The cache accumulated entries with no maximum size and no expiry, so resident
memory grew monotonically until the container was killed at its 2 GB heap
limit. Garbage collection could not reclaim entries because they remained
reachable from the cache map.

The deployment succeeded and canary health checks passed. The failure was time
delayed by approximately ten hours, which is why the deployment was not
initially suspected.

## Impact and blast radius

Infrastructure:
- 6 of 6 `orders-api` pods OOM killed over the incident window.
- Average pod lifetime fell from 30 days to between 8 and 18 minutes.

Services:
- `orders-api` degraded, intermittent 502 responses at the gateway.
- `payments-api` unaffected.
- `checkout-api` unaffected.
- `auth-gateway` unaffected.
- `notification-worker` unaffected.

Users:
- 3,400 users affected.
- 9% of order history requests failed intermittently.
- No write path was affected, so no orders were lost or corrupted.

Requests:
- 5,900 failed requests.

Data:
- No permanent data loss. The service is read-heavy for this path.
- 0 orders lost.
- 2 duplicate order confirmation emails sent, cosmetic only.

## Timeline

- 2026-03-03 16:20 - `orders-api v2.9.1` deployed. Canary health checks pass.
- 2026-03-04 02:00 - Heap usage observed at 400 MB, within normal range.
- 02:47 - First OOM kill. Incident declared SEV-2.
- 02:52 - Engineers observe a new error signature not seen previously.
- 02:55 - Attempt to increase the container memory limit from 2 GB to 4 GB.
- 02:58 - Second OOM kill. Memory increase not yet rolled out.
- 03:05 - Engineers correlate the crash signature with the most recent
  deployment and locate the unbounded cache.
- 03:14 - Third OOM kill. Incident escalated, SEV-2 to SEV-1 discussed.
- 03:31 - Fourth OOM kill. Decision made to roll back.
- 03:40 - Rollback of `orders-api v2.9.1` to v2.9.0 initiated.
- 03:58 - Rollback complete on 3 of 6 pods.
- 04:12 - Rollback complete on all 6 pods. Heap stabilizes at 480 MB.
- 04:30 - Incident closed.

## Attempted actions

1. Increase of the container memory limit from 2 GB to 4 GB, at 02:55 UTC.
2. Restart of all `orders-api` pods, at 02:58 UTC.
3. Rollback of `orders-api v2.9.1` to v2.9.0, at 03:40 UTC.

## Failed actions

- Increasing the container memory limit from 2 GB to 4 GB was attempted at
  02:55 UTC. It did not resolve the incident and was not completed. Because
  the cache is unbounded, a larger heap only delays the OOM rather than
  preventing it, proportionally to the size increase. This action was
  correctly abandoned at 03:05 once the unbounded cache was identified.

- Restarting all `orders-api` pods at 02:58 UTC did not resolve the incident.
  Restarting cleared resident memory and every pod ran cleanly for a short
  period, but each repopulated the cache from the ongoing request traffic and
  the pods were killed again at 03:14 UTC, 03:31 UTC and 03:49 UTC. Restart
  is a recurring pattern here rather than a fix, and treating each new crash as
  a fresh incident is misleading because the underlying growth curve never
  changed.

- Increasing the pod replica count from 6 to 12, proposed at 03:14 UTC, was
  rejected without being executed. It was correctly identified as spreading
  the same leak across more pods, which would exhaust database and cache
  backend capacity sooner and reduce the time before the next crash rather
  than extending it.

## Successful actions

- Rollback of `orders-api v2.9.1` to v2.9.0 at 03:40 UTC was the action that
  resolved the incident. Heap usage stabilized at 480 MB immediately after the
  final pod completed rollback at 04:12 UTC and no further OOM kills occurred.

## Relevant runbook

`memory-pressure-triage` (runbook id `memory-pressure-triage`) was consulted
at 03:20 UTC. Its guidance to check the most recent deployment before
increasing resources is what redirected the investigation from resizing toward
rolling back, and it is the step that resolved the incident.

## Lessons learned

- A health check that passes at deploy time says nothing about a leak. The
  failure was ten hours after deployment, well outside any canary window.
- Restarting repeatedly during a leak is a way of resetting the clock, not
  fixing the growth rate. Four engineers-hours were spent on restarts before
  the deployment was examined.
- The correct instinct is to check the most recent deployment before
  increasing resources, because a resource increase is only appropriate when
  the working set is genuinely larger than the limit.
- Rollback took 32 minutes to complete across 6 pods and could have been
  completed in under 10 with a faster rollout strategy. This is the largest
  avoidable cost in the incident.

## Related incidents

No directly related incidents were identified at the time of writing. This is
the first recorded unbounded in-memory growth incident in `orders-api`.

## Prevention and follow-up

- P0: Add a mandatory size bound and TTL to every in-memory cache introduced
  in this service, enforced in code review.
- P1: Alert on heap growth rate, not only on heap utilization, so a monotonic
  climb is detected hours before the OOM kill.
- P1: Require a soak period of at least 4 hours in staging for changes that
  introduce caching.
- P2: Add a heap profile capture to the OOM kill path so the growth source is
  available at crash time rather than reconstructed afterwards.
- P2: Investigate progressive rollout to reduce rollback time.
